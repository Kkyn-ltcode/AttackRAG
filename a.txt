1. Hallucination ground truth — you're right, this is the #1 risk.

We can't just "check if a claim is hallucinated" without knowing what the truth is. Here's the concrete solution:

We construct claim-level ground truth from the dataset labels we already have:

DARPA TC tells us: "Alert X was caused by attack scenario Y, which used techniques A, B, C"
CICIDS tells us: "This flow is a DDoS attack" or "This flow is benign"
We feed the alert to the system, extract every claim it makes, then check each claim against the known ground truth
Example: System says "This alert indicates T1059.001, likely APT29." Ground truth says the DARPA TC scenario was a Drakon APT simulation. The T1059.001 mapping might be correct (grounded AND accurate), but the APT29 attribution is hallucinated (grounded in ATT&CK — APT29 does use T1059.001 — but wrong for this specific incident). This gives us claim-level labels.

We can also create synthetic hallucination tests: deliberately test alerts where we KNOW the answer and count fabricated details the LLM adds.

This needs to be designed BEFORE we build the pipeline. I'll add a ground truth annotation spec to the plan.

2. Contribution 3 — agreed, it's thin.

"GI degrades under poisoning" is a thermometer reading, not a medicine. We need an active defense. Concrete proposal:

Provenance-Anchored GI: Every KG edge carries an immutable provenance tag (MITRE-official vs. community vs. auto-ingested). When computing GI, we don't just score relevance — we require that at least one supporting path passes exclusively through MITRE-official edges. Poisoned edges (which can only enter via lower-trust channels) can never fully ground a claim by themselves. This is a structural guarantee, not just a score adjustment.

3. High GI ≠ Accurate — the sharpest concern.

You're absolutely right. GI measures "is this claim supported by the knowledge base?" not "is this claim true for this specific incident?"

The honest answer: GI is necessary but not sufficient for correctness. We need to clearly define two metrics:

GI = groundedness (does the KG support this claim?)
Triage accuracy = correctness (is the final verdict right?)
The paper must explicitly say: "A high-GI claim that is grounded but incorrectly applied to the specific alert is a reasoning error, not a hallucination. GI eliminates fabricated claims; triage accuracy measures correct application of grounded knowledge."

This distinction actually strengthens the paper — it shows intellectual honesty that reviewers respect.
