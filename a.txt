1. The DARPA TC + CICIDS approach works for technique-level labels (T1059.001 correct/wrong). That's solid. The synthetic hallucination tests are also good practice.
The gap is APT attribution claims. Your own example exposes it — "APT29 attribution is hallucinated but grounded in ATT&CK." DARPA TC scenario labels won't reliably tell you which actor attribution claims are wrong, because the simulations don't always map cleanly to named threat actors. If your system makes actor-level claims, you need a principled way to label those as correct or incorrect, otherwise that claim category either gets excluded from evaluation or gets hand-waved.
The concrete question you need to answer before building: Will you evaluate actor attribution claims, and if so, what's the labeling source? The cleanest solution is to simply scope GI evaluation to technique-level claims (where DARPA TC gives clean labels) and explicitly state actor attribution is out of scope for this paper. That's not a weakness — it's honest scoping.

2. Your Provenance-Anchored GI requires every KG edge to carry an immutable provenance tag at ingestion time. This means your KG construction pipeline becomes a trust boundary — and that design needs to be specified upfront. If you're auto-ingesting CVE or community threat intel feeds, the provenance classification logic matters. A reviewer could ask: "What stops an attacker from spoofing provenance metadata at ingestion?" You don't need to solve this fully, but you need one paragraph in the paper acknowledging it as a threat model boundary.
