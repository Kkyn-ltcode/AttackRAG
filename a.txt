1. Actor attribution — agreed, scope it out.

You're right that DARPA TC scenarios don't map cleanly to named threat actors. The clean decision:

In scope for GI evaluation: technique-level claims (T1059.001), tactic-level claims (TA0002 Execution), CVE references, and D3FEND recommendations — all have verifiable ground truth from DARPA TC labels + KG structure.
Out of scope: actor/group attribution claims (APT29, Lazarus Group, etc.)
In the paper: "We scope GI evaluation to technique-level and mitigation-level claims, where DARPA TC scenario labels provide clean ground truth. Actor attribution verification requires threat intelligence provenance beyond the scope of this work."
This actually tightens the paper. One well-evaluated claim category beats four poorly-evaluated ones.

2. Provenance spoofing — agreed, acknowledge as threat model boundary.

The honest position:

MITRE ATT&CK and NVD are editorially curated by US government agencies (MITRE Corporation, NIST). We treat these as trusted by definition — the same trust assumption every IDS paper makes when using CVE data.
Community/auto-ingested feeds are explicitly lower-trust in our provenance scheme. That's the whole point of the trust score.
We do NOT claim to detect provenance spoofing at ingestion time. If an attacker compromises the KG ingestion pipeline itself, that's outside our threat model.
One paragraph in §3 (Threat Model): "We assume the integrity of upstream knowledge bases (MITRE ATT&CK, NVD). Compromising these sources requires subverting their editorial processes, which we consider out of scope. For lower-trust sources, Provenance-Anchored GI limits their influence by construction."
